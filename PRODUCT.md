# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People working on real-life goals (gym, study, side projects, habits) who respond to stakes and social accountability more than to streaks and badges. They check the app the way traders check a terminal: several short, glanceable sessions a day. Their friends are on it too, holding shares in each other.

## Product Purpose

A stock exchange where the listed companies are people. Your consistency is your fundamentals: hit your goals and your share price climbs; go quiet for a week and the market sells you off. Friends invest earned currency in each other, collect dividends on long streaks, and bet on each other's follow-through in a predictions market. Success looks like a user thinking "I can't skip leg day, my shareholders will kill me."

Core mechanics: opening/closing bells (08:00 to 00:00), candlestick charts, market caps and volumes, ticker symbols per user, a live trading floor feed, leaderboards, sector indices, IPOs for new users, dividends for consistency, monthly earnings reports with Bullish/Bearish/Hold reactions, public goals that work like corporate guidance, and shareholder pressure (comments, notifications when you miss).

## Brand Personality

Dry, precise, financial. The app never cheers, congratulates, or exclaims; it reports. Price action carries the emotion. Copy reads like a wire service: "EMMA falls 8.2% after a third missed workout." Wit is allowed only through deadpan market language applied to ordinary life.

## Anti-references

- Gamified habit apps (Habitica, streak apps): no XP bars, badges, mascots, confetti, or emoji.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: no pastel cards, gradient CTAs, rounded-friendly illustration.
- Cheerleader fitness-app voice: no "You've got this!", no exclamation marks, no congratulation toasts.
- Anything a user would identify as AI-generated design or copy.

## Design Principles

1. Report, never cheer. The interface states facts and prices; the user supplies the feeling.
2. Density is respect. Traders want numbers per square inch, not whitespace per card.
3. The market metaphor is load-bearing, not a skin. Every feature must behave the way its real-market counterpart behaves.
4. Red must hurt. Losses are shown as plainly as gains; softening the downside kills the stakes.
5. Alive by default. Tape moves, prices flash, the feed prints; a still screen means the market is closed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Direction is never color-alone: every gain/loss pairs green/red with a sign and arrow. Tabular numerals throughout. Full keyboard focus states. prefers-reduced-motion stops the tape, flashes, and feed animation.
